Monkey Punch – Privacy Policy

Monkey Punch ("the game") is a single-player arcade game for Android and the web. This policy explains what the game stores, what Google's services and the web leaderboard collect, and why.

What the game stores

  • Game settings and progress are saved on your device or in your browser. The web game also saves your analytics choice locally. Web leaderboard names and scores are stored on our server as described below.

Ads (Android)

The Android game shows ads from Google AdMob in one of two ways, never both: if you use the optional Lucky Spin, a rewarded ad that you choose to watch adds ten spins once your free daily spins are used; otherwise a full-screen ad plays between runs about once every twenty runs. To show and measure ads and to prevent fraud, Google may collect your device's advertising ID, IP address (and an approximate location derived from it), and information about ad interactions and app performance. In the EEA, UK and Switzerland the game asks for your consent first through Google's consent form; you can change your choice at any time with the AD PRIVACY button on the title screen. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID in your device settings.

Google's use of this data is described in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services and the Google Privacy Policy.

Analytics (Android)

The Android game uses Google Analytics for Firebase to understand how the game is played, for example how many people play each day, how long sessions last and which screens are opened, and to spot crashes. Firebase collects an app-instance identifier, your device's advertising ID, device model and OS version, approximate location (country/region derived from IP address) and these usage events. We only see aggregated reports. In the EEA, UK and Switzerland this follows the consent choice you make in Google's consent form. See Privacy and Security in Firebase.

Crash reports (Android)

If the Android game crashes, Firebase Crashlytics sends a crash report to Google so we can find and fix the fault. A report contains the crash stack trace, the game version, device model and OS version, a Crashlytics installation identifier, free memory and disk space, and a short log of what the game was doing just before (for example the current screen, chosen hit, costume and flight distance). It contains no name, contact details or advertising ID. Crash reports are kept for 90 days. See Privacy and Security in Firebase.

Analytics (web)

The web game uses Google Analytics for Firebase, connected to the same Analytics property as the Android app, only if you select Allow analytics. Before that choice, no Analytics SDK is loaded and no Analytics events are sent. Selecting No thanks leaves Analytics off, and the game and leaderboard still work. You can change your choice at any time using Analytics settings on the web page.

When enabled, Google uses Analytics cookies and a pseudonymous browser identifier to measure visits, sessions, browser/device information, approximate location and gameplay events: game readiness and leaderboard opens; selected hits and spins; launch power and aim choices; flight distances, durations and endings; timing opportunities and results; costume ability use; and unlocked hits. These events include game version and a run number within the current page session so we can compare aggregated gameplay results. We do not send your monkey name, leaderboard identity cookie or query strings to Analytics. Google advertising signals and ad personalisation are disabled for this web integration. Turning Analytics off stops collection and removes Analytics cookies available to this site; it does not erase data already received by Google. See Privacy and Security in Firebase.

Web leaderboard and server logs

If you name your monkey, we store that public name, your best and qualifying worst flight distances, and your lifetime banana total on our server. A separate random identity cookie lets your browser update those scores; only a hash of its secret is stored in the database. Scores can wait in browser storage while you are offline. The leaderboard cookie and score storage are used independently of optional Analytics. Our web server also keeps ordinary request logs, which can include IP addresses and request details, for operating and troubleshooting the site.

Global leaderboard (Android)

If you use Google Play Games, the game sends your distance at the end of each run to Google Play Games so it can appear on the global "World Top" leaderboard, and your shortest qualifying flight on the "World Loser Board", with your Play Games profile name and avatar. This is handled by Google under the Google Privacy Policy; you control your Play Games profile and visibility in the Google Play Games app.

If you use Google Play Games, the Android game also sends your saved lifetime banana total to Google so it can appear on Banana World with your Play Games profile.

What we do not collect

  • We do not ask for your email address, phone number or contacts. The web leaderboard uses a public monkey name chosen by you.
  • The web version has no ads. Web Analytics is optional.
  • Analytics retention is controlled by the linked Google Analytics property settings.

Deleting your data

  • Uninstalling the game, or clearing its app data or your browser's site data, deletes locally stored progress and settings. Clearing browser data also removes your Analytics choice and web leaderboard identity, but does not delete server-side scores or Analytics data already collected.
  • Android leaderboard scores and your Play Games profile can be managed or deleted in the Google Play Games app. For removal of a web leaderboard entry, contact us with its public monkey name.
